The sequence

Six poses, in this order

The game blends between your poses and adds small movement (a lean, a short step toward the target), so each frame should be a clear, held pose rather than an in-between. The widths below are proportional to how long each pose is on screen.

1 · Ready100 msLow combat ready, eyes on target; bridge from idle.
2 · Raise100 msWeapon rises through a clear intermediate pose.
3 · Brace130 msAnticipation: shoulder the rifle, bend knees and brace; other attacks coil or charge.
4 · Fire60 msRelease toward screen-right; muzzle flash, energy or melee strike.
5 · Recoil90 msAbsorb the shot or swing; distinct follow-through before lowering.
6 · Recover120 msSettle toward idle. Blend the transition back to default.

Size on screen: a figure is about 60–120 px tall on a phone. The pose has to read as a silhouette at that size: keep the limbs and weapon clear of the body, and push the Brace pose further than feels natural at full size.

Technical

The canvas

Foot baseline, y = 756. The lowest pixel of the feet sits 12 px above the bottom edge in every frame. The game pins this line to the board tile, so a frame whose feet sit higher or lower makes the character jump.
Center line, x = 256. The body stays centered on the same point in all six frames. Only weapons and effects reach past it.
Safe area: 24 px from each side, 18 px from the top. Nothing, including effects, may touch the canvas edge.
Quick check: lay default.png over each frame at 50% opacity. The feet should line up and the body should be the same size.
RequirementDetails
FilePNG, RGBA, 512 × 768 px, transparent background. One file per pose. Please also send the layered source (PSD, Krita, or similar).
FacingThree-quarter view, attacking toward screen-right, like the current figures. The game mirrors the frames for attacks to the left, so avoid lettering or one-sided details that would look wrong flipped.
ScaleThe body matches default.png exactly. Do not crop, re-center, or rescale frames one by one.
StyleMatch the current figure: the same painted, semi-realistic rendering, outfit, palette, lighting direction, and level of detail.
No groundNo floor, base, ground shadow, or background. The game draws the base and shadow.
EffectsMuzzle flash, energy, water, or dust may be painted into the Fire frame (with a trace of smoke or dust allowed in Recoil), attached to the figure and inside the safe area.
OutfitDefault outfit only for this commission. Outfits follow later, re-dressing the same poses (see “Outfits”).

Looking ahead

Outfits: your six poses become the pose reference

MythCards will add cosmetic outfits. Each outfit gets its own six attack frames, so a character never changes clothes mid-attack. Rather than new choreography per outfit, each outfit re-dresses the same six poses: the same silhouette, timing, and moment of impact.

  • The six poses you deliver now become that character's pose reference. Keep the layered sources; future outfits start from them.
  • Outfit frames use the outfit id as a prefix: aurora-rite-attack-1-ready.png … aurora-rite-attack-6-recover.png, in the same folder.
  • Zoya's Aurora Rite and Thalassa-Nekh's Drowned Seraph are the first outfits. They come in a later outfit pass, not this commission.
From the Yuri pilot

What the first delivery taught us

  • Pose 4 is the impact. The game shows the target being hit at the first moment of pose 4, so the shot, blow, or burst must land there, not in pose 3 or 5.
  • Keep attention on the target. The head and eyes stay aimed screen-right through anticipation, impact and recovery. A twisting torso must not make the character look behind, upward, or toward the viewer. Reactor Worker's first pass was corrected for this.
  • Keep the holds. The six durations above (600 ms in total) are the default for every character. If an attack needs different timing, say so with the delivery; the game reads the holds per character.
  • Keep carried props where the idle figure has them. At the end of the attack the game fades back to default.png. A prop may move when the pose needs it (Yuri's helmet went from under his arm to his hip, and that played fine); flag it with the delivery so we can check the fade back to idle.
  • Send a contact sheet and a preview GIF with each character, as the pilot did. They make review fast.
  • Attacks straight up or down the board reuse the sideways poses (decided 2026-10-15). Draw screen-right only.

Sign-off

Acceptance checklist

  • Six PNGs per character, named as above, 512 × 768, transparent; total hold duration 600 ms.
  • Feet on y = 756 and the body centered on x = 256 in every frame; matches default.png at 50% overlay.
  • Nothing touches the canvas edge (24 px sides, 18 px top).
  • Attacks toward screen-right; nothing reads wrong when mirrored.
  • Brace and Fire read clearly as silhouettes at 80 px tall.
  • The release (shot, blow, or burst) is in pose 4, Fire.
  • Same outfit, palette, and rendering as the current figure; no ground or shadow.
  • Layered source file, contact sheet, and preview GIF included.

Automatic check: on arrival, the game's test suite checks every frame for the canvas size, the foot line, the margins, the six poses, and the impact on pose 4. A frame that fails comes back with the exact file and problem.
