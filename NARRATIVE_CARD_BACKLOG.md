# MythCards Narrative And Card Backlog

## Purpose

This document captures the current story direction, visual/narrative guidance, faction sub-area structure, and proposed card backlog discussed in story planning. It is intended as handoff material for updating the playable prototype cards in a separate implementation thread.

The cards listed here are not all currently implemented. Treat this as a content source of truth for deciding which character, relic, and event cards should exist.

## Author And Format

Byline persona: **The Unnamed Archivist**

Format: **illustrated lorebook** with card-game artifacts.

Narrative frame: a hybrid of dream chronicle and occult war archive. The story is assembled from dream transcripts, classified Soviet records, recovered card images, personal confessions, battlefield fragments, myth annotations, and impossible artifacts.

Reality level: mostly realistic. The Closed City, people, injuries, relationships, secrecy, and technical programs should feel grounded. Mythic and occult elements should enter as serious forces whose exact nature remains intentionally unresolved.

Tone target:
- occult science disaster
- mythic war archive
- adult emotional motives
- dark and light themes
- relationships, intimacy, desire, guilt, loyalty, jealousy, grief, tenderness, and power as core drivers of action

Narrative rule for major characters:
- Public Role: what the faction or world thinks they are
- Private Hunger: what they actually want
- Relational Wound: who shaped, broke, or haunts them
- Forbidden Attraction: person, power, idea, or myth that tempts them
- Line They Will Not Cross
- Line They Eventually Cross
- Catch Phrase

## Visual Style

Core visual identity: **painted occult graphic novel / illustrated lorebook**.

Style pillars:
- mythic realism
- grounded historical, technological, and cultural detail
- heavy ink shadows
- textured brushwork
- cinematic lighting
- occult diagrams, sacred geometry, maps, constellations, reactor schematics, bloodline charts, and mythic symbols embedded into scenes
- characters shown as active story figures, not neutral portraits
- card art should feel like one panel from a larger graphic novel

Avoid:
- generic fantasy armor overload
- clean superhero comic style
- random in-image text
- lore dumps baked into art
- treating real-world-inspired material as caricature

Profile page art rule:
- profile pages should include artwork, character name, and quotes only
- no game stats, no abilities, no faction tags, no UI buttons, no gameplay numbers
- game data and stats should be added by the application

Card art rule:
- card art should not include text unless specifically requested
- leave usable negative space for future app/card frame overlays

## Core Story: The Closed City Incident

The story begins in a Soviet closed city in the 1980s. The city is officially unnamed or hidden under a postal designation. It contains three connected programs:

- Nuclear Research Facility: reactor testing, energy research, anomalous radiation studies
- Cosmonaut Signal Program: orbital telemetry, deep-space listening, symbolic structure in cosmic noise
- Consciousness Laboratory: dream monitoring, pilot/cosmonaut cognition, shared symbolic environments

The accident happens because these three systems synchronize when they should never have been connected:

- reactor pulse
- orbital receiver
- dream monitors

At 02:17 local time, instruments in different wings begin producing the same symbol. Sleeping subjects speak the same word. The first transcript renders it as **Atlantis**, though the Archivist treats this as suspect.

Official report:
- minor reactor irregularity
- localized radiation exposure
- electrical fire
- several injuries
- one scientist in coma
- no off-site contamination
- all records sealed

Actual event:
- the reactor does not explode outward
- it opens inward
- for seventeen seconds, the facility exists in more than one symbolic reality

Witnessed fragments:
- a drowned city behind reactor glass
- snow falling upward in a hallway
- a woman made of blue-white crystal in the signal room
- dead soldiers reflected in blank monitors
- a white tiger walking past sealed doors
- ocean pressure cracking concrete walls that never become wet
- a bronze sun over black water

Dr. Mikhail Orlov is found unconscious with circular light-burn markings on his palms.

Dr. Elena Morozova is not found.

Immediate aftermath:
- Orlov remains in a coma
- staff begin sharing dream landscapes
- children draw Atlantean pylons in school notebooks
- soldiers hear old battlefield songs on secure radio
- snow appears indoors near Orlov's hospital bed
- the signal array receives transmissions while powered off
- the first MythCards artifact appears beneath Orlov's pillow

### Nikolai Yegorov

Sub-area: Russian / Closed City

Card role: Common / Reactor Worker

Role in story: maintenance worker assigned to the lower reactor service corridors. Nikolai is not part of the classified dream program, but he knows the facility through its pipes, cables, pressure valves, locked doors, and practical failures. When Elena's dream work and the cosmonaut remote-viewing array begin feeding symbols into reactor diagnostics, Nikolai is one of the first people to realize the accident is not only nuclear. He sees frost form around conduit seams before the alarms sound, hears voices through dead intercom lines, and finds hand-drawn symbols scratched into panels he repaired the night before.

Public Role: reactor maintenance worker.

Private Hunger: to get his younger sister transferred out of the Closed City before the place consumes everyone inside it.

Relational Wound: his family depends on the same state system that traps them.

Forbidden Attraction: the dream signal, because it shows him places beyond fences, maps, and assigned lives.

Line He Will Not Cross: he will not abandon ordinary workers to save only scientists and officers.

Line He Eventually Crosses: he opens sealed service routes during containment, letting the breach spread but saving trapped people.

Catch phrases:
- "Machines tell the truth before men do."
- "If there is a door, someone built it. If someone built it, I can open it."

## Faction And Sub-Area Structure

Each faction has three story sub-areas. Each sub-area should eventually include:

- 1 Common
- 1 Mount
- 1 Warrior
- 1 Leader
- 1 Hero
- 1 Specialist
- 1 Mystic
- its own relics
- its own events

This creates 21 character cards per faction before variants.

## Russian Sub-Areas

### Winter Front

Story identity: army setting around World War II, siege warfare, frozen battlefields, sacred banners, battlefield ghosts.

Visual style: military coats, snow, icons, war maps, trenches, ruined cities.

Mechanical identity: endurance, defensive positioning, last stands, formation buffs, attrition.

### Far North

Story identity: remote northern wilderness, hidden villages, white animals, shamans, exiles, circus survivors, aurora gates.

Visual style: ice forests, iron birch, northern lights, animal spirits, traveling performers.

Mechanical identity: mobility, frost, ambush, spirit bonds, evasive movement.

### Closed City

Story identity: 1980s Soviet science and industry, nuclear power, leaks, cosmonauts, forbidden programs, consciousness experiments.

Visual style: concrete, reactor glow, warning lights, lead glass, telemetry, occult diagrams hidden in technical systems.

Mechanical identity: risk/reward effects, radiation or leak markers, placed objects, overloads, signals, delayed effects.

## Atlantean Sub-Areas

### First Mind

Story identity: early Atlantis at the height of mental, spiritual, and cosmic knowledge.

Visual style: star maps, dream architecture, psychic councils, living geometry.

Mechanical identity: prediction, card manipulation, shared actions, telepathy, range through linked minds.

### Crystal Dominion

Story identity: later Atlantis with great crystals, power grids, resonance engines, pylons, and empire before collapse.

Visual style: quartz armor, obelisks, harmonic weapons, energy channels, luminous cities.

Mechanical identity: pylons, shields, range boosts, resonance networks, board control through structures.

### Flood Survivors

Story identity: post-flood Atlanteans who survived underwater, underground, or migrated into Egypt and mystery traditions.

Visual style: drowned temples, sealed chambers, subterranean routes, Egyptian influence, memory stones.

Mechanical identity: survival, hidden movement, recursion, relic preservation, return-from-defeat effects.

## Future Major Areas

Narrative role: blood, inheritance, curses, the dead refusing to stay symbolic.

Visual style: Carpathian mountains, moonlit forests, black iron gates, candlelit chapels, red velvet, burial soil, old coins, alchemical notes, bloodline trees.

Possible sub-areas:
- Noble Bloodline / vampire aristocracy
- Carpathian folk villages / hunters / witches
- Monastery and burial crypts / saints, relics, revenants

Mechanical direction: blood costs, curses, inheritance, resurrection, charm, nocturnal movement, oath mechanics.

### Greek Mythology

Narrative role: fate, heroism, divine cruelty, prophecy, transformation.

Visual style: sunlit marble, bronze armor, storm seas, temple shadows, oracle smoke, labyrinth geometry, constellations over ruins.

Possible sub-areas:
- Olympian heroic age
- Underworld and chthonic mysteries

Mechanical direction: prophecy, heroic trials, divine favors, fatal bargains, transformations, monster objectives.
