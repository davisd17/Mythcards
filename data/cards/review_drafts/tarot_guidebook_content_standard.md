# Tarot Guidebook Content Standard

Status: approved shared instruction

Purpose: define the content boundary between the physical tarot cards and the companion guidebook so narrative, design, and implementation work stays consistent across threads.

## Physical Card Face

The face of every physical tarot card contains only:

- the card artwork
- the traditional tarot card name and its canonical number or rank

Example: `XVII - THE STAR`

Do not place the MythCards character/event/relic name, quotations, affirmations, keywords, lore, game statistics, ability text, sub-area labels, or explanatory copy on the physical tarot card face.

## Guidebook Entry Order

Use this order for every guidebook entry:

1. Traditional tarot name and number/rank
2. MythCards character, event, or relic name
3. Affirmation
4. Three upright keywords
5. Three shadow/reversed keywords
6. Brief narrative section appropriate to the card type
7. Quote or flavor line appropriate to the card type
8. Short interpretation connecting the MythCards subject to the traditional tarot archetype
9. `When Drawn` guidance

## Writing Rules

- Keep the narrative brief and intriguing. Reveal enough to establish the subject and conflict, but leave unanswered questions that encourage readers to seek the larger story.
- Write affirmations in first person, normally 4–12 words. They should be emotionally usable without becoming generic positivity.
- Use exactly three upright and three shadow keywords unless a later deck-wide editorial decision changes the standard.
- Treat `shadow` as the card's difficult, blocked, distorted, or reversed expression. Do not make every shadow meaning purely evil.
- Keep the tarot interpretation grounded in both the traditional archetype and the actual MythCards narrative.
- End with practical `When Drawn` guidance. It should advise the reader without pretending to guarantee an outcome.
- Do not include game statistics or prototype mechanics in the guidebook entry.

## Card-Type Variations

### Character Cards

- Label the narrative section `Backstory`.
- Use a direct quotation spoken by the character.
- The quote should reveal personality, desire, fear, contradiction, or worldview while reinforcing the tarot archetype.
- Keep the backstory very brief, generally 2–3 sentences.

### Event Cards

- Label the narrative section `Event` or `What Happened`.
- Use an omen, remembered line, command, witness statement, or short narrative flavor line instead of a character quote when appropriate.
- Focus on the decisive moment and why it changes the story.

### Relic Cards

- Label the narrative section `Legend` or `Provenance`.
- Use an inscription, warning, ritual phrase, archival note, or remembered saying instead of a character quote.
- Explain what the object means and why people desire, fear, protect, or misuse it without reducing it to game mechanics.

## Approved Character Example

### XVII - The Star

#### Nadia Belaya

**Affirmation:**  
*I remain open to hope after hardship.*

**Upright:** Hope | Healing | Renewal  
**Shadow:** Distance | Idealization | Unspoken Need

**Backstory:**  
Nadia finds Pavel dying in the northern snow and heals him with medicine, prayer, and an unexplained power. Before he can learn why she expected him, she disappears.

**Quote:**  
*"Hope is not a promise that you will be spared. It is the reason you rise after you are not."*

Nadia embodies the Star's restoration after catastrophe. Her disappearance also reveals its shadow: hope can guide us, but idealizing its source may prevent us from seeing the real person behind it.

**When Drawn:**  
Accept the hope being offered without turning its source into something perfect. Healing has begun, but you must choose what to build from it.

## Editorial Checklist

Before approving an entry, confirm:

- the physical card still requires only artwork and the traditional tarot identity
- the affirmation is first-person and specific to the card
- the six keywords do not simply repeat the prose
- the narrative creates curiosity rather than summarizing the entire plot
- a character quote sounds like that individual character
- the interpretation clearly explains why this MythCards subject maps to this tarot archetype
- `When Drawn` gives concise, useful guidance
- no game rules or statistics have entered the guidebook copy
